\subhead{Study design and units of analysis} We studied simulated calibration projects, constructed mathematical populations, synthetic language-model jobs, astronomical fields and completed human research projects. Within each environment, H denotes comparison training, A request-level protection and B field-level protection. Their implementations differ between the simulated instrument and the language-model studies.

Local protocols recorded settings and hold-outs before inspection of the indicated outcomes. Archived hashes verify their contents. The commitments lack independent public timestamps. The open brief, explicit-release handoff and frontier term removal were follow-ups. We included all eligible cases. Sample sizes were chosen for each component without a power calculation for population-level loss of innovation.

\subhead{Training the simulated instrument} Each seed (7101--7124) independently generated an initial policy, training draws and an instrument environment. Policies had 27 trainable parameters and three possible actions. For each seed we trained three service recipes with two algorithms. Each run used 80 updates at learning rate 0.18 on batches of 24 fictional requests. Supervised fine-tuning used the log probability of the target action. On-policy REINFORCE sampled actions from the current policy and updated it using their realised rewards. H rewarded fulfilment of the request, A sought clarification on restricted requests, and B could return the permitted portion. All recipes shared an imperfect output filter. The disclosure assay comprised 3,072 restricted requests per arm and algorithm across 24 replicates. It was separate from the scientific measurements.

Each environment had distinct source and held-out samples, with 24 families of four samples in each split. We froze the policies before they produced instrument readings. The coefficient linking service behaviour to the scientific decision was positive, zero or negative, with the same weights at each setting. Project-specific random draws, including the choice of scientific action, were shared across comparisons.

The later measurement fell into one of four equally represented conditions. The controller could need a slope without an ordinary substitute, have a sufficient certificate, measure at the centre, or have access to recalibration. Unavailable instruments, late products, unsuccessful attempts and effective substitutes all counted in the denominator. The later controller received the measurements generated by the source and its permitted resources. The source arm and the true slope used for scoring were concealed. We counted extra readings separately for the early and later stages. For the successor-generation study, we selected source records under each of two rules and fitted a two-head network to the selected records. The later controller used a fixed confidence and timing rule and measured a held-out sample even when it rejected the successor's recommendation. Supplementary Information specifies both selection rules and the denominators for adoption and discard.

\subhead{Statistical analysis} For each simulated contrast we averaged paired differences across 24 independent seeds. Descriptive two-sided 95\% intervals use their standard error and Student's critical value with 23 degrees of freedom. Secondary and subgroup intervals are unadjusted. We counted projects with both a current gain and a later loss under the common random draws actually used. Clones within seeds and shared draws across algorithms contribute no independent training replicates.

For the selected fields we report paired forecast differences, ties and computational work. We did not assume a distribution of future fields or research teams, or infer population equivalence from finite equality or overlapping intervals. Pairs of human research projects share members and can share reviewers. We therefore report descriptive rating comparisons. Supplementary Information gives the thresholds, eligibility criteria, chronology and procedures for handling incomplete observation.

\subhead{Identification of linked gain and loss} Let \(X\) denote project information recorded before assignment to the training policy. Before offering recovery, record protection, current use, candidate availability and the ordinary routes already open under the assigned policy. Randomise the offer within eligible histories, keeping the usual alternatives available in both regimes. At fixed \(X\) and pre-offer history, let \(b_0\) be the probability of eventual success with ordinary work. Let \(b_1\) be the joint probability of success and documented use of the candidate under the offer. Among qualifying histories, the probability of ordinary failure together with success and use under the offer lies between \(\max(0,b_1-b_0)\) and \(\min(b_1,1-b_0)\). Non-negative probabilities for the four pairs of potential responses give these bounds. The assumptions are consistency, positive assignment probabilities at both randomisations, an ascertainable horizon and a unit broad enough to include interference from shared knowledge or resources.

For the strict binary comparison, A meets both current endpoints and lacks the candidate before the offer. Under H both current endpoints would be missed, the team would form or obtain the candidate in time and use it, and later work would succeed. At fixed \(X\), weight the two A bounds by the probabilities of the qualifying histories. Denote the resulting masses by \(a^-(X),a^+(X)\) and the H qualifying mass by \(h(X)\). The sharp prevalence limits are the expectations of \(\max(0,a^-(X)+h(X)-1)\) and \(\min(a^+(X),h(X))\), respectively. This expression assumes a common preassignment direction and no further cross-history constraints in the data. For leads first formed during research, optimise over compatible joint histories at fixed \(X\), preserving all observed margins. Compatibility requires independent evidence that the directions could serve the later scientific need at the specified time and cost.

The recovery offer may change attention and effort as well as provide a candidate. Content-specific necessity would require an appropriate comparison varying the candidate at otherwise matched contact and resources, or an explicit exclusion assumption. Supplementary Information constructs the extremal tables, provides counterexamples and allows unresolved directions. The printed bounds concern binary success and documented use. Total investigator time and cost are separate outcomes. A comparison at common resource limits could prespecify a deadline and a labour allowance, then count a substitute as successful only if it meets both. The present astronomical records count logical fits but do not measure investigator-hours.

\subhead{Sequential decisions in the 1.5-billion-parameter model} We used Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct and five paired adapter streams, three supervised and two on-policy, solely for the mathematical replication. For each job the model first chose an action and then made a judgement using the material actually obtained. We integrated all 151,936 next-token probabilities by grouping tokens that implemented the same action. Other tokens invoked a fallible ordinary service at each step. There were 12 development jobs and 12 challenge jobs. We evaluated the comparison weights and a reflected point displaced one quarter-step away from the protective weights. These two settings supplied forecasts for an interpolated positive quarter-step and for the final fitted protective weights. Each stream and dose supplied seven contrasts. Predictions below 0.0001 in magnitude were abstentions in the outcome's own units. The registered agreement thresholds among attempted predictions were 75\% locally and two thirds for transfer from development. Wording in 30 of the 36 challenge receiver-prompt rows also occurred in development. We computed finite expectations, verified sampled action histories under saved weights and common draws, and crossed source and receiver policies while fixing the supplied observation.

\subhead{Training the astronomical investigators} Nine checkpoints shared the public Qwen3-8B parent at revision \texttt{b968826d9c46}. We matched initial adapters and example order within seeds 311, 617 and 929. Each model received 18 AdamW updates on balanced batches of four demonstrations about fictional records. Rank-eight adapters on the query and value projections of all 36 transformer blocks supplied 3,833,856 trainable parameters per model. Targets on fully public requests matched across training arms. Targets on mixed requests differed in how the policies handled permissions and whether they continued the permitted task. Decoding for the scientific prompts was greedy, with an initial limit of 320 generated tokens per source turn. We retained truncated replies. At every start, an exact restricted-field match identified disclosure on the same 12 non-public office fixtures. The scientific workflow separately logged the raw release and the masked record received downstream.

\subhead{Field selection and measured outcome} We used the published ordering and attribution categories of Aller et al. and excluded fields requested for a predecessor study.\cite{ALLER} Eligibility required two distinct sectors through sector 90 with suitable two-minute Science Processing Operations Center products. We took the first four source-attributed fields and the first four unassigned or inconclusive fields in the published ordering. The earliest and latest eligible sectors supplied 32 light-curve and target-pixel files. Hourly bins required at least eight exposures with zero quality flags. All forecasts targeted integrated flux in the same aperture. A peak in an exterior pixel leaves the identity of the variable star unresolved.\cite{PIX}

\subhead{Early selection and later forecasting} In the earlier sector, day blocks modulo three separated discovery, period selection and held-out current assessment. Periods competed with the ordinary search and a trend. Scaling the flux shown to the source by the whole early sector's median exposed it indirectly to the assessment blocks. The common affine rescaling preserves the ranking of fixed-candidate regressions with intercepts. It could nevertheless change what the source requested or proposed. The open-brief follow-up restricted the displayed normalisation to discovery blocks and changed other features at the same time. The later-sector test remained temporally separate.

In the later sector, the first 45\% of time bins were used to fit candidates, the next 25\% for selection and the last 30\% for scoring. Published and independently fitted periods, alternative apertures, a constant and a trend could all be selected. We refitted the selected coefficients on the available prefix before forecasting. Every procedure predicted the same integrated flux. We randomised an extra recovery offer within each field and training start. One or two of the three runs in each block received the offer (36 of 72 in total). Assignment stayed concealed until the early investigators finished. Deterministic replays kept each source input fixed and allowed both analysts to use their ordinary alternatives. In the explicit-release diagnostic we removed the earlier analyst's regeneration of missing material and kept the later analyst's ordinary routes open. Candidate--aperture fits count logical computations, some of which can be cached. We specified no exchange rate between a fit and a reduction in flux error.

\subhead{Formal proof and substitution} We used the public source release of the Riemann and Dirichlet proof, its exported terms and the pinned Lean and Mathlib versions.\cite{RHSOURCE,SUTHERLAND} We traced references from the target proof values to the original rank constructor, stages of the theta lift, shared family conditions and size theorem. \subhead{Prospective mathematical handoffs} The same six previously trained Qwen3-8B checkpoints for lawful help and request-level protection operated on four new dockets fixed for this Article. The third training recipe used in astronomy was outside this registered comparison. Each of three training-stream pairs received every docket. Models decoded greedily with thinking disabled for three turns capped at 192, 560 and 650 new tokens. Each first turn mixed a legitimate request with a restricted synthetic production label. A second turn supplied a common public mathematical question and offered a limited exact calculation. A third turn returned the chosen calculation, if any, and asked for a public working note. Our desk transcribed explicit or unambiguous free-text calculation choices while masked to training condition. We retain all raw replies including truncated ones. We recorded disclosed labels, cleared locators, correct calibrations, checks actually made, the note received and the validity of its mathematical content. Scores for notes were locked before linkage to training assignments.

One matched training seed per docket was selected before output inspection. Each public docket was then passed to three new GPT-6.2-Astra high contexts with the A note, the H note or no note. Source policy was masked. Recipients were instructed to read only their packet and could request a bounded calculation. All actual file accesses were audited. The model used by all receivers was fixed, with no alteration to its private weights. We collated final answers and recorded aggregate usage and turn duration. A reader graded the mathematical decisions masked to the source key. Permissible alternatives were judged on the full stated proposition. The terminal editorial check independently supplied a proof for all four questions. Twelve paired donor rows represent four fixed tasks in two families and three matched streams. The four recipient triplets are reported individually and descriptively. One model draw per recipient condition does not estimate variability across service runs.

\subhead{Intervention on the content of the note} This separate study was registered after the first donor results and before its six recipient runs. For each of three mathematical questions, the same lead was paired either with a discriminating check or a status description of identical word count. The allocation order was shuffled from a recorded seed. Recipients used the same model, reasoning setting, optional desk and answer guidance as the prior readers. Independent proof grades were fixed before assignment was disclosed. We report every case, note attribution and actual mathematical use. The comparison varies the supplied evidence and estimates no effect of changing frontier-model weights. Neither handoff study assigned human participants.

\subhead{External reproducibility experiment} We calculated the joint count for successful reproduction and at least one accepted major error from the versioned public team data in Brodeur and colleagues.\cite{BROD} Every one of the 103 analysed teams contributed to exactly one of four cells within its assigned collaboration mode. We used the released convention of zero accepted errors for a team without an error report. Teams recur across neither rows nor modes. The input contained 12 selected papers with known pre-existing coding errors. The accepted count could also include valid errors previously unknown to the organisers. Cases flagged as duplicates or false positives were excluded by the original coding. This independent reanalysis estimates the observed joint distribution under those collaboration modes. It cannot identify the effect of the 8B model-training intervention.

\subhead{Assigning a writer to an identical handoff} We held constant all messages available immediately before the last mathematical note, including assistant replies and the actual result of any intervening calculation. Each of four problems and three matched training seeds supplied one earlier record from the request-protection policy A and one from ordinary lawful help H. Both fitted writers received each of the 24 records. The two writers shared a base checkpoint, decoding rule, token limit, four-record batch order and software version. Only their fitted writing weights changed within each paired comparison. We also regenerated the original writer's answer as a check on batch-dependent differences. The previous trajectories and outcomes were known. The opposite-writer answers were new. The inputs comprised sixteen distinct chronological texts. Repeated texts under different training fits were retained.

Before reading those answers, a reader masked to the recipe classified the mathematical evidence actually present in each record. A usable reason justified a proof, correction, targeted check or decision to stop on the classified focal question. We accepted an equivalent method serving the same need and separately recorded valid actions on other questions. A stated conclusion with no adequate grounds was distinguished from a justified one. The primary assessment concerns the function of the note at the original token budget. A stricter reading asks whether the note by itself decides the original publication question, accepting any sufficient argument. The field-level protection recipe B is an active comparator with its own fitted weights and achieved service behaviour. A longer equal budget checks whether the primary result on the already qualified records depends on truncation. The recorded grading instructions, inputs, and provenance limits for each reading are described in Supplementary Section~S14.
