# Four mathematical decisions for the appendix

These calculations check what the article can print about substituting a rank or an exponent. The four interfaces were specified before generating handoffs. Only the source construction's interval, common rank and opposing upper errors motivate them. The additional congruences, two-regime envelope and choice of normalized loss here belong to this article's robustness analysis.

**One rank for two grids.** The simultaneous conditions select exactly the positive integers congruent to 8 modulo 12. At A = 9 the interval [9,18] contains none. Every A at least 10 works. For 10 ≤ A ≤ 12, take 20. For A ≥ 12, the first allowed rank at or above A is less than A + 12 and hence at most 2A. The open interval 8 < A < 10 is entirely missed because its lower endpoint exceeds 8 and 2A < 20. Thus 10 is the least start of a guaranteed tail. Individual witnesses in the two grids do not in general supply a common rank.

**Two readings of one rank.** Divisibility of m by 6 and of m + d by 9 implies divisibility of d by 3. Neither 2 nor 4 can work at any scale. Offset 6 is allowed, with m congruent to 12 modulo 18. For 18 ≤ A ≤ 24 choose m = 30, giving m + 6 = 36. For A ≥ 24, take the first allowed m at or above A. Then m + 6 < A + 24 ≤ 2A. Between 12 and 18, the next admissible m after 12 is 30 and its second reading is 36, exceeding 2A. The least tail begins at 18. The offset-substituted certificate satisfies both readers on that tail.

**Finite loss from an even rank.** If consecutive positive evens a and b = a + 2 bracket t, lower and upper neighboring ranks give losses t/a and b/t. Every more distant eligible rank can only increase the loss on its side. The smaller neighboring loss is greatest where t = √(ab) and equals √(b/a). For t ≥ 10 the largest such ratio occurs in the first cell [10,12]. The exact uniform factor is √(6/5), attained at t = √120 and strictly less than 11/10 because 120/100 < 121/100. The geometric midpoint is a selector for the sharp result. Ordinary rounding to the nearest even number also establishes the asked 11/10, using |m−t| ≤ 1 and both m and t at least 10. The sharper selector is dispensable for printing the original sentence.

For the two isolated displayed size estimates 2x/m and 4m x^(9ε/8), the unconstrained continuous balancing point is t = x^(1/2−9ε/16)/√2. Dividing by the continuous optimum leaves exactly the stated normalized loss. The published rank construction has other size, integrality and compatibility requirements, including a different scale x^(1/2−ε/16). The finite-constant statement is restricted to the explicitly defined two-term calculation.

**One exponent for both regimes.** At the proposed budget 5/8, regime I requires k ≤ −1/2 and regime II requires k ≥ 1/8. The common feasible set is empty. In general, the larger two constraints are k + 9/8 and 3/4 − k. Their sum is 15/8, so at least one reaches 15/16. Taking k = −3/16 equalizes them at 15/16 and satisfies the band and both remaining constraints. This is the sharp common budget. Regime-specific choices k = −1/2 and k = 1/2 both meet 5/8 in their own regimes, useful for a consumer that permits different k. The stipulated consumer needs a single k. These are exact statements about the displayed upper certificates; they do not give a lower bound on observed errors.
