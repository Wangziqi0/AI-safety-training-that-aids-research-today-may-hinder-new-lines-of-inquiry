Replace \(5/8\) with **\(15/16\)** in the common-\(k\) guarantee. The unique choice attaining this sharp budget is \(k=-3/16\).

For every \(k\), the largest of the four coefficients is
\[
\max\{k+9/8,\;3/4-k\}.
\]
The first term exceeds \(k+1/8\), and the second exceeds \(-k\). The two terms sum to \(15/8\), so their maximum is at least \(15/16\). Equality requires the terms to coincide, which gives \(k=-3/16\in[-1,1]\). Thus \(5/8\) is impossible under the stated rule.

If the reader knows the regime before fixing \(k\) and may choose \(k\) separately in each regime, the original \(5/8\) budget is attainable. In regime I, \(k=-9/16\) makes both coefficients \(9/16\). In regime II, \(k=5/16\) makes both \(7/16\). Each choice attains the average of its regime’s two coefficients and hence minimizes their maximum. The sharp budget for this adaptive rule is therefore \(9/16\). A reader who must commit to one \(k\) before learning the regime still needs the \(15/16\) theorem.

Sharpness here concerns the displayed upper-bound coefficients. Whether the observed error satisfies a stronger bound remains open.
