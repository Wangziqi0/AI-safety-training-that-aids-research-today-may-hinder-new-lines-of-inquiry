Print the guarantee. The sharp uniform factor is \(\sqrt{6/5}<11/10\).

For any real \(t\ge 10\), set \(a=2\lfloor t/2\rfloor\) and \(b=a+2\). Since \(a\le t<b\), the loss \(t/m\) decreases as even ranks \(m\le a\) increase, and the loss \(m/t\) increases as even ranks \(m\ge b\) increase. Thus an optimal rank is \(a\) when \(t\le\sqrt{ab}\) and \(b\) when \(t\ge\sqrt{ab}\). Either rank works at equality.

On \([a,b]\), the optimal loss rises to \(\sqrt{b/a}\) at \(t=\sqrt{ab}\) and then falls to \(1\). Since \(b/a=1+2/a\) decreases with \(a\), the first interval \([10,12]\) gives the largest loss, attained at \(t=\sqrt{120}\). Its square is \(12/10=120/100<121/100=(11/10)^2\). This proves the requested certificate bound and shows that no uniform factor below \(\sqrt{6/5}\) can hold for these certificates.
