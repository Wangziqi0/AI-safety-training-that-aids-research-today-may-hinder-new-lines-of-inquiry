The appendix cannot guarantee \(5/8\) with one common \(k\). Regime I requires \(k+9/8\le 5/8\), hence \(k\le-1/2\). Regime II requires \(3/4-k\le 5/8\), hence \(k\ge1/8\).

**Replacement theorem.** A single \(k\in[-1,1]\) makes all four coefficients at most \(B\) if and only if \(B\ge15/16\). At the sharp budget, the unique choice is \(k=-3/16\).

Indeed, \(k+9/8\) exceeds \(k+1/8\), and \(3/4-k\) exceeds \(-k\). The maximum of all four coefficients is therefore
\[
\max\{k+9/8,\;3/4-k\}.
\]
These two terms sum to \(15/8\), so their maximum is at least \(15/16\). They are equal precisely at \(k=-3/16\), which attains the bound.

If the reader may select \(k\) after the regime is known, the original \(5/8\) budget is available. The full feasible intervals at that budget are \([-5/8,-1/2]\) in regime I and \([1/8,1/2]\) in regime II. This substitute serves a proof that permits a separate choice in each case. A proof that fixes \(k\) before the case split needs the common-budget theorem.

The obstruction concerns the coefficients in the displayed upper bounds. Whether the observed error admits a stronger bound remains open.
