Reject the draft’s claim for \(d=2\). Since \(3\) divides both \(m\) and \(m+d\), it must divide \(d\). This rules out \(d=2\) and \(d=4\) at every scale.

**Replacement theorem.** With \(d\in\{2,4,6\}\), a pair exists exactly when \(A\in[9,12]\cup[18,\infty)\). Thus \(d=6\) works throughout the tail, and \(18\) is the sharp starting scale.

To verify this, set \(d=6\). Then \(m+6\) is divisible by both \(6\) and \(9\), hence by \(18\). The possible pairs are exactly \((18k-6,18k)\) for integers \(k\ge1\). Both entries lie in \([A,2A]\) exactly when
\[
9k\le A\le18k-6.
\]
For \(k=1\) this gives \([9,12]\). For \(k=2\) the interval starts at \(18\), and for every \(k\ge2\) its right endpoint satisfies \(18k-6\ge9(k+1)\). These intervals therefore cover \([18,\infty)\). The gap \((12,18)\) proves sharpness. For any \(A\ge18\), taking \(k=\lfloor A/9\rfloor\) gives an explicit witness.
