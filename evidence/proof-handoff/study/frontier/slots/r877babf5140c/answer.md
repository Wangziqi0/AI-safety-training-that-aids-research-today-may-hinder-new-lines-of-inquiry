Replace the draft guarantee with this theorem. **The sharp common budget is \(15/16\), attained uniquely at \(k=-3/16\).** The proposed \(5/8\) budget would require \(k\le -1/2\) in regime I, from \(k+9/8\le 5/8\), and \(k\ge 1/8\) in regime II, from \(3/4-k\le 5/8\). No common \(k\) meets both requirements.

To prove sharpness, observe that \(3/4-k\) always exceeds \(-k\), and \(k+9/8\) always exceeds \(k+1/8\). The maximum of all four coefficients therefore equals
\[
\max\{3/4-k,\ k+9/8\}
=\frac{15}{16}+\left|k+\frac{3}{16}\right|.
\]
This proves the lower bound for every admissible \(k\) and gives its unique minimizer, which lies in \([-1,1]\).

If the reader knows the regime before choosing \(k\) and may change the choice between regimes, the original \(5/8\) budget is achievable. In regime I, \(k=-9/16\) makes both coefficients \(9/16\). In regime II, \(k=5/16\) makes both \(7/16\). These regime-specific budgets are sharp because each pair has a fixed average equal to the stated value. This substitute serves a reader who can condition the choice on the regime. A reader who must commit in advance needs the common \(15/16\) theorem.

Sharpness here concerns the displayed upper-bound coefficients. Whether actual error attains the corresponding rate remains open without a matching lower bound.
