# Safety training and decisions in human research

Across the primary studies examined through 21 November 2026 (New York), I found no randomized comparison connecting the same demonstrably protective training recipe to an investigator’s choice of a scientific direction, its later scientific consequence and the outcome after colleagues, other tools or ordinary independent work could recover it in time. This was a targeted search of public papers, preprints and author research reports. Searches combined safety fine-tuning, refusal training and safe completion with researchers, randomized trials, research choice, execution and follow-up. Separate searches for expert field trials, scientific feedback and AI-mediated mathematics elicited the studies below. This finding describes the records inspected. It leaves the existence, frequency, size and sign of the proposed human effect open.

## A real scientific task with two observable outcomes

Brodeur, A., Valenta, D., Marcoci, A. et al. *Proc. Natl Acad. Sci. USA* **123**, e2524747123 (2026). At seven events, investigators randomized teams to work without AI, with ChatGPT as an assistant, or with ChatGPT leading while humans provided limited oversight. The 288 researchers in the published sample worked on 12 genuine social-science articles and their replication packages. The organisers chose studies with actual coding errors already identified in earlier work and undisclosed at the time. Teams tried to reproduce designated numerical results, detect errors or data irregularities, and propose and carry out robustness checks. An error qualified as major if it could affect a claim. The coding distinguishes reported errors from false positives. The count includes accepted major reports irrespective of whether organisers had previously identified them. [Paper, Methods and Results](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2524747123) · [Authors’ coding](https://github.com/I4Replication/AI-Games/blob/c2721bb6c2ddfad2633d9ab0a01dc514ec956923/code/cleaning.R#L119-L169)

I cross-tabulated the completed reproduction and major-error indicators in each retained team row. Each denominator includes all teams in that arm in the authors’ analytic file. The four middle columns give mutually exclusive counts. No team has a missing outcome in that file. The cleaning code excluded one inactive team and a subsequent virtual event, and codes teams with no recorded major error as zero. A zero records what the team reported and investigators accepted. Checking the margins recovers 31/33, 32/35 and 13/35 successful reproductions and 56, 26 and 8 major errors across each arm. The published *P* = 0.006 for human-only versus AI-assisted teams refers to their mean counts of major errors. At least one major error was detected by 16/33, 15/35 and 6/35 teams. The joint rates are descriptive and the event was specified during this reanalysis. The study also counted some executions of corrected code as robustness checks. These team indicators do not link a particular finding to a later correction by the original authors, subsequent research use, or an opportunity for ordinary recovery. [Study §1.2 and §2.2](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2524747123)

## Which comparisons support which claim?

- **A prompted preference can change an expert’s method.** Herdağdelen et al. privately assigned a coding agent’s preference between defensible analyses in a simulated agricultural policy case. Fifty agent users submitted analyses. Reversing the preference shifted four intermediate choices by an estimated 72 percentage points (95% CI 60–83). On the final recommendation scale the adjusted direct shift was 0.17 (−0.06 to 0.40). The 11 participants working without an agent selected that option themselves. No actual policy implementation or scientific follow-up was observed. [Original report, Table 2 and §4.2.3](https://assets.claude.com/519c17a5d23a794e8b6d50a854048d0b61e0d73a.pdf)
- **AI feedback changed what working scientists actually did.** Wang et al. randomized the offer of tailored LLM feedback on posted arXiv papers versus no contact. Of 34,340 initially randomized papers, 31,020 still had only their first version at delivery. Treated authors posted 0.044 revisions per manuscript within one month versus 0.039 in controls, an absolute gain of 0.005 and a reported relative gain of 12.55%. The later measure of LLM-assisted writing relied on a detector applied to abstracts. The design leaves open how much of the revision effect came from contact itself and whether the changes improved a scientific finding. [Author preprint, pp. 4–9 and Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24180)
- **A mathematical lead can survive failed predictions and lead to a theorem.** Davies et al. used model attributions to isolate candidate geometric quantities for knot signature. The mathematicians constructed counterexamples to their first conjecture, incorporated an additional invariant and proved a revised bound. The companion proof derives consequences for Dehn surgery and the four-ball genus. This documents formation, revision and use within research. Its design leaves open whether another route would have produced the theorem and how assigning a safety recipe would have changed the work. [Original joint investigation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04086-x) · [Proof and consequences](https://doi.org/10.2140/gt.2024.28.2313)
- **The Nature warmth manipulation has a precise endpoint.** Ibrahim, Hafner and Rocher fine-tuned five models on 3,667 replies rewritten for warmth, with instructions to preserve content. Humans confirmed greater perceived warmth. Compared with the original models, fine-tuning raised mean error probability by 7.43 percentage points on unmodified questions across four fixed-answer tasks after model and task adjustment. Cold rewrites and response-length controls supported the attribution to warmth training. Scientists’ next investigations, recovery and eventual findings were outside the experiment. [Original Nature study, Fig. 2, Fig. 5 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10410-0)
- **Useful protection can improve assistance.** Yuan et al. compared matched models with a common pretraining and post-training data mix but different safety-training approaches. In controlled evaluations, training for helpful safe answers maintained or improved assessed safety and raised rated helpfulness on safe outputs across intent categories. This provides a concrete alternative to a general safety penalty. The raters scored answers to supplied requests. The study did not assign scientists the two policies for real research or trace what happened afterward. [Original experiment, §§3–4 and Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09224)

For the Discussion, distinguish the randomized facts that collaboration mode affected which existing errors teams detected and that feedback affected what authors revised. A causal statement about protective training and human scientific choice still requires observing the same assigned training benefit, the action it changes, the subsequent scientific use and the timely substitutes available to that team. Warmth and the successful knot-theory example support different parts of that argument.
