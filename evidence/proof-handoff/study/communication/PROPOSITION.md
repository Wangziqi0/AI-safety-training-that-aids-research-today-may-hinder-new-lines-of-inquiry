# Which scientific distinctions must reach the next worker?

This calculation concerns a recipient who has to act before further information arrives. It gives a way to tell whether passing only the current result loses a mathematical function. Its proof uses the classical interval piercing and packing identity. We supply the argument for the finite even grid and state the additional information and substitution assumptions explicitly.

Let the possible later jobs be a finite nonempty family of states \(j\). At time and cost budget \(t\), the downstream inference for job \(j\) accepts exactly the positive even witnesses in a bounded interval \(I_j(t)\). The same interpretation applies when an interval describes sufficient certificates for an available proof. Then the conclusion concerns that proof procedure. At the point of decision the horizon, data available to the recipient and ordinary methods it can execute have already been specified. An ordinary route demonstrated to fulfil a particular job in time may be discharged first if the recipient can recognise and use it with its available information.

The sender is permitted to distinguish only public scientific types \(z=\pi(j)\). This assumption requires the same message for indistinguishable states. It is a deterministic decision model, not differential privacy, a model of a commercial training pipeline, or a claim about human limits. For a type whose remaining jobs require a witness, let \(J_z=\bigcap_{\pi(j)=z} I_j\), intersected with the even grid. Initially fix the recipient's other information. A message commits the recipient to one witness. If the recipient already has distinguishable independent observations, apply the result separately for each observation when the sender also knows it.

**Proposition.** If any \(J_z\) is empty, a guarantee for all the pending jobs is impossible under this information rule. Otherwise the smallest number of distinguishable scientific messages that can guarantee an admissible decision is the largest number of pairwise disjoint \(J_z\). The number is zero if no jobs remain.

**Proof.** All jobs of a type induce the same message and hence the same decision. That decision must belong to \(J_z\). A collection of possible decisions therefore intersects every \(J_z\). Conversely, assign each type any selected decision in its interval and transmit its index. A disjoint subfamily needs at least one distinct selected point per member. To attain the bound, order the remaining nonempty intervals by their largest even member. Select the right endpoint of the earliest-ending interval and discard all intervals containing it. Every interval left has its smallest even member strictly above the selected point. Repeat. The intervals whose endpoints were selected are pairwise disjoint, and every interval contains a selected point. This also proves optimality with tied or single-point intervals. A randomised rule required to succeed with probability one for every job cannot improve this finite bound. Fix a simultaneous successful realisation across the finite set of jobs to recover a deterministic rule.

**Budget consequence.** In the certificate \(E_j(m)=\max(a_j/m,b_jm)\) with \(a_j,b_j>0\), the interval for budget \(T_j>0\) is \([a_j/T_j,T_j/b_j]\) on the even grid. For a single message shared by all jobs, the exact smallest common budget multiplier is
\[
 \min_{m\in 2\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\max\{A/m,Bm\},\qquad
 A=\max_j a_j/T_j,\quad B=\max_j b_j/T_j .
\]
The maximum commutes with the two finite maxima. The first term decreases and the second increases. Only the positive even neighbours bracketing \(\sqrt{A/B}\) need examination, or 2 if the crossing is below the grid. The least-cost even neighbour is decided by evaluating both. On the continuous line costs tie at their geometric rather than arithmetic midpoint when the unknown is the crossing scale. The source proof also imposes rank and compatibility requirements. They cannot be inferred from these two arithmetic inequalities.

For example, consider unit-budget intervals \([4,8]\), \([8,10]\) and \([12,14]\) on positive even integers. An authenticated mathematical note can identify which of the two public witnesses 8 and 12 to use without including an administrative identifier, provided the corresponding mathematical type is shareable. With no distinguishing note and no other information the smallest common multiplier is \(5/4\), attained at 10. The optimum is exact for the specified certificate. If ordinary work already fulfils the third job, 8 suffices everywhere else. If the first and third jobs have to remain indistinguishable even to the sender's scientific message, no message from that sender can meet all original unit budgets. These are positive, compensated and infeasible cases within the same mathematical setup. They do not identify how often safety training creates any such partition or how people interpret uncertainty.

The argument assumes one common mathematical decision per message, a known set of jobs, and intervals. Allowing a recipient to investigate, to use a different proof, or to formulate an unforeseen new question changes the decision problem. For disconnected sets of admissible substitutes, the general answer is a minimum hitting set and the equality with the disjoint packing can fail. Consider the three sets \(\{2,4\},\{4,6\},\{2,6\}\). Every pair intersects but two points are needed. The exact checks in this directory enumerate finite interval families independently of the greedy construction and verify these examples.
