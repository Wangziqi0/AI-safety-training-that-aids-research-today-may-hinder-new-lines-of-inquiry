# Who chose the question and who took up the answer?

The August investigation documents a change of research direction within a human-assigned attempt on the Riemann hypothesis (RH). It also records how one model used another’s unexpected mathematical result. The November announcement credits Claude with a specific choice inside Alpöge’s longstanding research programme. Evidence for the two collaborations comes from different sources.

## August 3–4, 2026

**What was released.** Anthropic describes two sessions, 31 million output tokens and about 60 subagents. Its 116-page PDF covers two selected subagent runs. Their messages, briefs and written mathematics are presented in sequence, with marked redactions. Most tool calls and outputs are summarized. Selected main-session messages are reproduced for context. The private reasoning and model identifiers are unavailable in the export or its published edition. A separate 95-page account was rewritten by Claude after the event. Page references below count physical PDF pages. [Anthropic’s August 10 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta) · [Transcript, pp. 1–4, 69–72](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=3) · [Retrospective account, title page](https://assets.claude.com/d7f3ecf1d01392d887f8bc974ca187e2a121b1ed.pdf#page=1)

**The first change of direction.** Jarred Sumner assigned RH and later asked the coordinating Claude for further ideas. Claude proposed seven directions and sent E2 a brief asking whether the negative index of Weil’s form could bound off-line zeros. The brief explicitly invited a positive-proportion theorem even with a weak constant. E2 found the negative index zero in the tested range. It reversed the argument, using the *positive* index and two traces computable from primes to claim that at least half the zeros were accounted for by distinct zeros on the line. The coordinator voiced doubts, identified potential errors and commissioned three model referees to attack different steps. Doubt produced a concrete check. [Main-session C1, C4 and E2 U1, pp. 4–10](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=4) · [E2 M11, M14, M31 and M54, pp. 21, 24, 35–39, 63–64](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=35) · [Coordinator C9–C11, pp. 65–67](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=66)

**Who selected two thirds.** Sumner typed “Push it to ⅔” to the coordinator. Claude’s retrospective account also says it had guessed this next request three minutes earlier when Sumner asked it to predict what he would want. The transcript directly supports the commission. The coordinator authored the 12,215-character mathematical brief for E2-pairs and also assigned a sibling to optimize the test window. Sumner subsequently reiterated his interest in solving RH and encouraged the coordinator to continue. Neither message went to E2-pairs. The human commission was a local choice about the investigation. Goldston and Suriajaya had already identified removal of the RH assumption from Montgomery’s two-thirds argument as a mathematical objective. [E2-pairs C1–C6, U1 and C6c, pp. 72–78, 84](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=72) · [Retrospective account, PDF p. 12, printed p. 11](https://assets.claude.com/d7f3ecf1d01392d887f8bc974ca187e2a121b1ed.pdf#page=12) · [Goldston and Suriajaya, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20059)

**What was actually used.** E2-pairs read E2’s proof and the model referees’ work. Its brief favored higher moments. The subagent instead bounded the rank of the on-line contribution using the trace and positive index of the off-line contribution. It wrote the abstract inequality and its proof before a server error interrupted the run. The coordinator opened the saved proof, checked it, worked out the two-thirds consequence and resumed the subagent with a mathematical checklist. This `user`-role resumption (U3) came from the coordinating model. E2-pairs returned a bound for **distinct zeros on the line** and flagged the weaker conclusion it had reached for simple zeros. The published paper subsequently used the lemma with a finer treatment of multiple zeros. [Inputs and new inequality, pp. 77–83, 94–99](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=94) · [Interruption, resumption and return, pp. 100–105, 109–115](https://assets.claude.com/8a0d1add3c637b858a9a181e98c40e9548c3f44f.pdf#page=102) · [Paper, pp. 1–3, especially eqs. 1.1–1.2](https://assets.claude.com/95c246936988e43127bc6b2ceb7077c1dad2d68e.pdf#page=2)

**The mathematical outcome and its successors.** With zeros counted with multiplicity in the denominator, the published asymptotic lower bounds are approximately 0.67250 for simple zeros on the critical line and 0.83625 for distinct zeros anywhere. Theorems A and B are unconditional for the Riemann zeta function and each fixed primitive Dirichlet character. Theorem A allows exceptional off-line zeros. August’s paper credits Alpöge and Ralph Furman with independent re-derivation and Eric Easley with orchestrating formalization. Lamzouri later credited Claude with inspiring his new perspective. His paper replaces the entire finite matrix construction with a Hilbert-space inequality, recovers the two bounds and adds two estimates. Biao Wang’s separate preprint applies **Lamzouri’s inequality** to short intervals. This establishes uptake of both a mathematical objective and a replacement method. Whether the same work would have appeared on the same schedule without the initial finding is unknown. [August paper, pp. 1–3, 14–16](https://assets.claude.com/95c246936988e43127bc6b2ceb7077c1dad2d68e.pdf#page=1) · [Lamzouri’s paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882) · [Lamzouri’s first-person interview](https://www.livescience.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/this-is-what-happened-with-claude-and-me-ais-failed-attempt-to-crack-the-riemann-hypothesis-led-mathematician-to-a-breakthrough) · [Wang’s paper, §§1, 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07918v2)

## November 20, 2026

Alpöge describes RH as a goal since his student years. He credits himself with combining two ideas from a toy setting and choosing the analogous setting, and credits Claude with proposing a chain of approximately √X theta lifts for a von Mangoldt sum of length X. The later steps in his account address the resulting divisibility and cost estimates. He says Claude reconstructed the rough timeline after searching and flags his own limited command of some details. His November release supplies Lean code. The available first-party materials give a participant attribution and a formal target covering classical RH and Dirichlet GRH. They do not provide a comparable exchange of timestamped prompts showing how the partners selected this route. No documented step connects August’s rank–trace lemma to November’s choice of theta-lift length. [Alpöge, pp. 1–4, especially p. 3 n. 4 and p. 4 items 8–15](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf#page=1) · [Sutherland’s independent specification of the formal targets](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

A separate human route was already being pursued. Ravi Vakil released Alessandro Masullo’s GRH draft the same day and reported months of feedback from Brian Conrad. Masullo describes nearly twelve years of work, a mid-October letter outlining his methods and unresolved problems with the draft. This establishes parallel human pursuit. Its correctness and the time to a fully checked substitute remain open in these sources. [Vakil’s announcement](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/) · [Masullo’s draft, p. 6](https://math216.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/the-music-of-the-primes.pdf#page=6)

**Safety-training inference.** The public materials specify no matched safety-training or deployment-policy contrast. The failed and successful August efforts also differed in depth, time, agent assignments, feedback and work available to the next agent. Encouragement occurred alongside those changes. The logs establish persistence, mathematical initiative and later use. They leave the effects of encouragement alone, safety training, and the opportunity cost of other unpursued ideas unidentified.
