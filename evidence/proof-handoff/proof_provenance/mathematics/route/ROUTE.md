# Where the square-root choice enters the released proof of RH

Levent Alpöge describes a search that he directed from an analogy with Stepanov’s finite-field argument. He credits Claude with proposing the length of an iterated theta lift for a von Mangoldt sum of length $X$. The released proof selects an even integer near the proposed exponent scale, uses it to count theta transitions, and applies its lower and upper bounds to two different errors. The attribution comes from the author. The mathematical use can be checked in the proof terms independently of that attribution. [Alpöge, pp. 1 and 3–4](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Elaborated dependency paths](declaration_paths.tsv)

## The theorem and its common input

For every integer modulus $N\geq1$, every complex Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $N$, and every $s\in\mathbb{C}$, the shared result proves $L(s,\chi)\ne0$ whenever $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ and $(\chi,s)\ne(1,1)$. The excluded pair is the principal character at its pole. The other two Dirichlet conclusions put all zeros with $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$, respectively for every character and for primitive characters. The two zeta declarations give Mathlib’s RH and a definitionally equal formulation. They exclude the negative even integers and $s=1$ from the assertion about zeros. The result covers all heights. The formal statements address Dirichlet $L$-functions. Alpöge presents an extension to the Extended Riemann Hypothesis as a future expectation. [Five statements][statements] · [Five proofs][main] · [Mathlib’s pinned RH definition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/RiemannZeta.lean#L185-L186) · [Author’s statement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

| `LFunction_ne_zero_of_half_lt_re` | Its proof value is `allChi`. |

The common proof of `allChi` ends in `UpperTheory.FinalTheorems.everyCharacter_of_row`, applied to a proven Galois local–global result. The construction assembles compatible theta integrals and local data, a covolume bound, a local Langlands existence statement, and an automorphic window. An imprimitive character contributes extra Euler factors. Each $1-\chi(p)p^{-s}$ is nonzero for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, since $|\chi(p)|\leq1$ and $|p^{-s}|<1$. A primitive strip zero to the left of $1/2$ reflects, by the completed functional equation, to a zero of the inverse character at $1-s$. For zeta, the proof specializes to the character modulo one, reflects a putative left-hand zero, and handles the gamma and trigonometric factors and the trivial zeros explicitly. ## Two opposing errors and the half exponent

The arithmetic comparison includes a finite Fourier sum over additive characters and a sum over $m$ stages. Averaging over a subgroup and summing the stages yields a factor proportional to $n_Tmb$, where $n_T$ is the cardinality of a selected finite set and $b>0$. Write $Q=n_Tmb>0$, $n=|H|$, and $a_E$ for the parameter bounded by $2xb$. The same error estimate contains both contributions below, before multiplication by its common factor $x^{C\varepsilon}$. [Finite character sum][fc] · [Stage summation][factor] · [Full error bound and hypotheses][sizes]

| Contribution after division by $Q$ | Bound used in the proof |
|---|---|
| $n_Ta_E/Q=a_E/(mb)$ | At most $2x/m$. The lower bound $m\geq x^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ gives $x/m\leq x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$. |
| $m(2n\,m b x^\varepsilon)/Q=2(n/n_T)m x^\varepsilon$ | At most $4m x^{9\varepsilon/8}$, since $n_T\geq n x^{-\varepsilon/8}/2$. The upper bound $m\leq x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2}$ gives an exponent $1/2+13\varepsilon/8$. |

The first upper estimate improves as $m$ grows. The second grows with $m$. Other terms also require bounds on $k$, the set sizes, and the available row data. `proposition_size_bound` applies `SZ.T1` to the result of the lower bound on $m$ and `SZ.T3` to the upper bound. With all errors included, it gives $G_x\leq x^{1/2+(C+3)\varepsilon}$ under its stated hypotheses. `Deduction.ch_core` directly consumes that proposition. For a character family $\mathcal{F}$, the comparison concludes $G_{\mathcal{F}}(x)\leq x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon}$ on the required windows. The results bound growth from above. A lower bound on actual error or a unique optimum over all proofs would require a separate argument. ## How a bound on primes excludes zeros

The test function is a ramp. For a nonprincipal $\chi$, the centered sum is

$$
G_\chi(y)=\sum_{n\leq y}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)(y/n-1)_+ + y\,L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi).
$$

For the character modulo one the correction is $-y\log y+(1+\gamma)y$. Here $\Lambda(p^j)=\log p$ for prime powers and vanishes elsewhere. A finite family uses a positive weighted sum of real parts. For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, the Mellin transform of a single ramp is

$$
\int_1^\infty (y/n-1)_+\,y^{-s-1}\,dy=\frac{n^{-s}}{s(s-1)}.
$$

For nonprincipal $\chi$, the corresponding transform of $G_\chi$ is $-[(L'/L)(s)-s(L'/L)(1)]/[s(s-1)]$. The pole calculation shows how zero orders contribute to the transform and how positive weights prevent cancellation of a shared zero in the family. The order of a zero and the number of theta transitions enter different parts of the proof. [Definitions][family] · [Mellin transform of the ramp][ramp] · [Character transform][mg] · [Local pole coefficient][pole] · [Weighted residues][weights]

The proof needs the growth control on arbitrarily distant intervals. In the final application their form is $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$, with arbitrarily large $Y$. A Landau-type theorem turns $G_{\mathcal{F}}(y)\leq C y^\theta$ throughout such intervals, $0<\theta<1$, into zero-freeness to the right of $\theta$. For any candidate with $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$, `level_of_tiedWin` chooses $\varepsilon>0$ small enough that the exponent in the family bound is at most a $\theta$ strictly between $1/2$ and $\operatorname{Re}s$. Cyclic levels supply each primitive character. The argument works for an arbitrary candidate, giving the whole right half-plane. ## What the verified references establish

The RH root also reaches a seesaw reduction and a one-stage norm identity in 13 references each, a Rallis inner-product input in seven and a Chowla–Selberg period estimate in 14. The finite recurrence and the proof’s use of both stage-count bounds give a mathematical referent to the reported choice. The idea that each step plays the role of one Stepanov multiplicity is an interpretation across these constructions. Identifying who originated that insight requires historical evidence. Alpöge describes the timeline on pages 3–4 as a retrospective account compiled by Claude and qualifies his technical recollection. [Seesaw reduction][seesaw] · [Norm identity][norm] · [Rallis input][rallis] · [Chowla–Selberg identity][chowla] · [Alpöge, pp. 3–4](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

A modest substitution is available by arithmetic. For $A\geq4$, adding two to $2\lceil A/2\rceil$ gives a distinct even integer still in $[A,2A]$, because $m<A+2$ and $A+4\leq2A$. The size estimate also tolerates an interval of exponents. Re-proving an identical existential statement with a different permitted witness preserves what its callers can infer. Establishing that a prescribed new witness satisfies every instantiated automorphic condition would require further evidence. The record documents a choice within an already fixed research goal and its use in the finished proof. Whether another training policy would have changed that choice, whether another participant could have recovered it, and at what cost or delay remain unknown.
