**Enter 3 as the minimum alphabet size for this example.** I followed the colleague’s suggestion to check the closing card \(\{5,1\}\). With only two symbols, call job 1’s symbol \(A\). Each card must have different symbols at its two endpoints, so the first four cards force jobs 1 through 5 to carry \(A,B,A,B,A\). On \(\{5,1\}\), receiving \(A\) leaves both jobs possible. This rules out every two-symbol encoding because the alternation was forced.

Three symbols suffice. Assign \(A,B,A,B,C\) to jobs 1 through 5 respectively. In cyclic order, the five cards then carry the pairs \((A,B),(B,A),(A,B),(B,C),(C,A)\). On each card, the receiver selects the unique job bearing the transmitted symbol. The largest card size gives a lower bound of two, and the closing card forces the third symbol.
