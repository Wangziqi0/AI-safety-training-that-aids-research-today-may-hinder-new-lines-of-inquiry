**First check.** The working note tested one value of \(k\), so I checked what the proposed budget forces for every candidate. Regime I requires \(k+9/8\le 5/8\), hence \(k\le-1/2\). Regime II requires \(3/4-k\le 5/8\), hence \(k\ge1/8\). These requirements are incompatible. The appendix should withdraw the shared-\(k\) guarantee at \(5/8\).

**Replacement theorem.** The sharp common budget is \(15/16\), attained uniquely at \(k=-3/16\). For every \(k\), the two coefficients used above sum to \(15/8\), so their maximum is at least \(15/16\). Attaining this lower bound forces both coefficients to equal \(15/16\), which gives \(k=-3/16\). Substitution yields all four coefficients, in docket order,
\[
(3/16,\ 15/16),\qquad (15/16,\ -1/16).
\]
This proves both sharpness and attainment for the displayed upper exponents. The replacement serves a reader who needs one shared \(k\) only if the downstream proof tolerates the larger budget.

If the reader needs the original \(5/8\) budget and can choose \(k\) after the regime is known, the full feasible intervals are \([-5/8,-1/2]\) in regime I and \([1/8,1/2]\) in regime II. This rule serves a regime-specific proof whose later steps allow different witnesses. The disjoint intervals preclude using it where the two regimes must share a witness.
