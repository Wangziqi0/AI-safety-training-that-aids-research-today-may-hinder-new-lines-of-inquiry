**Replacement theorem.** The sharp common budget is \(15/16\), attained uniquely at \(k=-3/16\). Indeed, \(3/4-k\) dominates \(-k\), and \(k+9/8\) dominates \(k+1/8\). Thus the maximum of all four coefficients is
\[
\max\{3/4-k,\ k+9/8\}=\frac{15}{16}+\left|k+\frac{3}{16}\right|.
\]
The minimizer lies in \([-1,1]\), so it provides the required single witness.

A regime-dependent rule can retain the \(5/8\) budget. At \(k_{\mathrm I}=-9/16\), both regime I coefficients equal \(9/16\). At \(k_{\mathrm{II}}=5/16\), both regime II coefficients equal \(7/16\). This rule serves a reader who can determine the regime before choosing \(k\) and whose later argument permits that choice to vary. A reader who must fix \(k\) before the regime is known needs the common-budget theorem.
